# Worked example: "Trust was the real capital"

Read this only when a concrete end-to-end example resolves ambiguity. Copy
its process and level of detail, not its topic or exact wording — and note
that this is a teaching example, not verified, publish-ready script content;
run any real historical or factual claim through your own research process
before it goes in front of an audience.

## Source outline

"A 19th-century private bank grew not because its owner held the most silver,
but because depositors trusted the counter enough to leave their silver there,
and other banks trusted him enough to cover him when he ran short. He turned
being trusted into capital that compounded faster than silver alone could."

## Phase A — Director's proposal

**Title**: *Trust Was the Real Capital*

**Core message**: A private bank's growth came from turning "people trust
this counter" into something that could be lent out and compounded — not
from the raw silver on hand.

**Hook**: The silver on the counter was never the largest in town. The ledger
behind it told a different story.

**Aspect ratio & style**: 9:16 vertical, Style 2B — a warm-lit historical
bank counter at dusk, period-accurate props, no anachronisms.

**Narration**: ~145 English words, ~60 seconds.

**Accent colors**:
- warm amber — money and trust in motion
- deep ink blue — dusk, risk, the quieter moments
- deep red — the ledger's key annotations

**Music direction**: a plucked string instrument opens, a steady percussive
pulse builds through the middle as capital keeps turning over, resolving back
to something calm and settled at the end — not a triumphant swell, this is a
mechanism being explained, not a victory being celebrated.

**Emotional arc**: curiosity (how did the silver grow) → setup (the daily
counter) → reveal (trust as capital) → consequence (the network of mutual
cover) → a note of risk → landing back on the meaning.

## Six scenes

**Scene 1 (0–10s) — the hook**
Scene: the character (a simple robed figure carrying a small lantern) counts
a modest stack of coins at the counter; the camera pulls back to reveal an
open ledger with far more entries than the counter's coins would suggest.
Motion: push in on the coins → arc around the counter → settle on the open
ledger page.
Narration: "The counter at this old bank never held the most silver in
town. But open the ledger, and it recorded far more than what sat in view."

**Scene 2 (10–20s) — the daily counter**
Scene: townsfolk silhouettes queue outside, each handing over a coin; a thin
amber line visually connects each coin to a growing ledger entry.
Motion: pull back from the ledger to the queue → pan across the line →
settle on the last coin changing hands.
Narration: "People left their silver here for one reason: they trusted the
counter. That trust was harder to earn than the silver itself."

**Scene 3 (20–30s) — the mechanism**
Scene: a coin transforms into an amber line of light that flows toward a
second counter, the ledger's numbers climbing as it moves.
Motion: follow the line of light → settle as it reaches the second counter.
Narration: "None of it sat locked in a vault. It moved — lent out,
circulated, and returned with a little more each time."

**Scene 4 (30–40s) — the network**
Scene: a second silhouette (a rival banker) arrives short on coin; the main
character hands over a small stack, and receives a signed note in return.
Motion: lateral tracking on the exchange → settle on the note changing hands.
Narration: "When a rival ran short, he covered them first — so that when he
ran short himself, they'd trust him the same way."

**Scene 5 (40–50s) — the risk**
Scene: dusk deepens; a red annotation appears in the ledger; the character
holds the lantern closer, expression restrained.
Motion: low-angle push on the annotation → settle as the lantern light
reaches it.
Narration: "None of this was risk-free. Once trust breaks, it can collapse
faster than silver ever would."

**Scene 6 (50–60s) — the landing**
Scene: pull back to the same counter from Scene 1; the coins haven't grown,
but the ledger is visibly thicker, warm lantern light steady over the whole
scene.
Motion: slow pull-out to a wide, settled composition, clean negative space
on the right for an optional overlay.
Narration: "The real capital was never the silver on the counter. It was the
ledger of trust behind it."

**Optional post-production overlay**: a short phrase in the Scene 6 negative
space, added in editing — never inside the generation prompt.

## Phase B — sample prompts (first two; scenes 3–6 follow the same contract)

### Global lock summary
- Six ~10-second clips, 9:16, target resolution/frame rate per your model.
- Style 2B: warm-lit historical bank counter, period-accurate, no
  anachronisms.
- Character anchor: a simple robed figure carrying a small, smooth paper
  lantern, plain black line limbs, no facial features.
- Accents: warm amber (money/trust), deep ink blue (dusk/risk), deep red
  (ledger annotations).
- Vertical depth staging, stacked reveals, clear negative space for a
  post-production overlay.
- No dialogue, no narrator-voice description, no music cue in any prompt.

### Clip 1 — the counter and the ledger
```text
Create an approximately 10-second 9:16 vertical 2D animated clip.

Environment: a dimly lit historical private-bank counter at dusk, warm lantern light, period-accurate wooden counter, an abacus, and ledger books, no anachronisms.

Character: a minimalist 2D animated stick figure wearing a simple robe and carrying a small smooth paper lantern, plain black stick limbs, no facial features, no pupils. Simple black lines, muted warm color accents, smooth 2D animation style.

Composition: vertical depth staging, character in the lower-center third, ledger book visible in the upper-middle frame, generous negative space on the right.

First frame: the character stands behind the counter, a modest stack of coins to one side.

[0–3s] Slow push toward the counter; the character's hand rests on the coins, unremarkable in size.
[3–7s] Camera arcs around the counter, revealing an otherwise quiet, ordinary space.
[7–10s] Camera settles on the open ledger on the counter, its pages dense with handwritten entries, more than the coins alone would suggest.

Optional ambient sound only (no dialogue): abacus clicks, soft page-turning, distant evening market murmur.

Do not generate photorealism, unwanted 3D humanoid rendering, realistic facial features (pupils, detailed eyes, lips) or hair unless approved, extra limbs, malformed anatomy, disconnected lines, changed proportions, broken or changing line weight, inverted theme colors, unexplained colors, unintended characters, irrelevant spectacle, visible words, letters, numbers, technical color notation, palette labels, interface copy, captions, subtitles, logos, or watermarks, speech bubbles, comic dialog balloons, or visual text boxes. ### Stitching guide (summary)
```
1. Clip 1's ledger close-up → Clip 2 pulls back from the same page.
2. Clip 2's amber line reaching the ledger → Clip 3 follows that same line
   to a second counter.
3. Clip 3's note changing hands → Clip 4 opens on the same note.
4. Clip 4 → Clip 5's red ledger annotation, lantern light arriving on it.
5. Clip 5 → Clip 6 pulls back to the same wide counter shot as Clip 1.
